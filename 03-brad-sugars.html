01 — What he teaches

The method, decoded

Strip the "billionaire" branding and there are four frameworks doing the work. All four are geography-neutral ideas — his funding talk is US/SBA-flavoured, but the operating logic travels.

Buy · Build · Sell

The core thesis

Buy a slightly-profitable, established business (proven cash flow, customers, infrastructure) rather than start one. Install systems to lift the profit, then hold it for cash flow or sell at a multiple of the improved profit. His target screen: fragmented industries, ageing-owner demographics, roughly 15–25% margins — the same succession-gap hunting ground as our buy box.

The 5 Levels of Entrepreneur

A self-diagnostic — and a warning
LVL 1
Employee — does tasks.
LVL 2
Self-employed — owns a job, does everything themselves.
LVL 3
Manager — runs people and processes.
LVL 4
Owner — thinks in systems & scalability; buys the systems that generate cash. ← where we must operate.
LVL 5
Investor — buys purely for capital appreciation & portfolio.

His sharpest line: "Most failed acquisitions happen because buyers operate at Level 2–3 while trying to execute a Level 5 strategy." Translation for us below.

The 6 Steps to a business that runs itself

Mastery → Niche → Leverage → Team → Synergy → Results

His operating ladder for an owned business: get control of time & numbers (Mastery), sharpen the offer (Niche), systemise (Leverage), build the team (Team), make it run without you (Synergy), then bank the value/freedom (Results). The mantra: "systems run the business, people run the systems."

The Level 2 trap — aimed straight at us

With your trades background, the real risk is dropping to Level 2 (self-employed) — getting pulled into running the work yourself instead of owning the system. His whole point: we're buying a business precisely so we don't do that. Decide now who holds the "Owner" seat and protect it.

03 — The piece worth stealing

The 5 Ways: our profit engine after we buy

This is the bit our plan was thin on — how we grow the profit that services the debt and builds equity. It's one formula with five levers. Nudge each a little and profit compounds.

The formula
Leads × Conversion = Customers
Customers × Avg £ Sale × No. of Transactions = Revenue
Revenue × Margin = Profit
WAY 1
Leads
More enquiries. A retained cleaning/maintenance firm often does zero marketing — add a simple lead system and you've found free growth the old owner left on the table.
WAY 2
Conversion rate
Win more of the quotes you already send. Follow-up, better proposals, faster response.
WAY 3
No. of transactions
Get each customer buying more often — service contracts, planned maintenance, recurring reviews. Turns one-off jobs into an annuity.
WAY 4
Average £ sale
Raise prices (often under-priced by a retiring owner), bundle, upsell adjacent services.
WAY 5
Margin
Tighten costs, cut the unprofitable jobs, systemise delivery. Sugars says basic financial controls alone reveal 5–15% quick wins.
Why it matters for the debt

A 10% nudge on each of five levers isn't 10% more profit — it compounds. That headroom is exactly what turns a thin-margin, debt-loaded deal (the risk we flagged in Memo 01) into a comfortable one. This formula is the safety margin.

04 — Turned into action

Our first-100-days plan, his levers

Sugars' four post-acquisition value levers, as a checklist to run the day we complete.

DAYS 1–30
Financial controls first
Install real reporting — weekly numbers, job-level profitability, cash. This alone surfaces the 5–15% quick wins and tells you which of the 5 Ways to pull.
DAYS 1–30
Protect the team & the manager
Keep the people who run it; secure the second-tier manager. The whole deal rests on it not depending on the departed owner.
DAYS 30–60
Document the systems
Write down how the work actually gets done — the fastest route to removing owner-dependency and making it sellable/scalable later.
DAYS 60–100
Switch on marketing
Add the lead system the old owner never had (Way 1–2). Sugars claims this can double growth rate — the single biggest upside in a sleepy, referral-only SME.
ONGOING
Then repeat — bolt on the next
Same as our Memo 01 buy-and-build: a group worth more than the parts.

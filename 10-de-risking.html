De-Risking the Deal

The one lever behind all three

The structure of the deal is the master control. More seller finance + investor equity and less bank debt shrinks your guarantee, cuts your cash, and de-risks the whole thing — all at once. Fix that one thing first.

01

Minimise personal exposure

"how do we limit what's on the line?"

Exposure comes almost entirely from the personal guarantee on the bank loan. The game is to shrink it, cap it, and insure it — not to pretend you can avoid it.

  • 01
    Buy through a Newco
    The corporate veil means you're only personally exposed where you sign a PG — nowhere else.
  • 02
    Cap the guarantee
    Insist on a limited PG (a % or fixed sum, push for ~20%), never unlimited.
  • 03
    Several, not joint-and-several
    With your partner, each capped to your own share — so you're not liable for the whole if they can't pay.
  • 04
    Keep your home out
    Under GGS a lender can't take your principal residence. Don't volunteer other personal security either.
  • 05
    Shrink the guaranteed layer
    The PG attaches to the bank loan only. More seller finance + investor = smaller loan = smaller PG. The seller loan and investor carry no guarantee.
  • 06
    Insure it
    Personal Guarantee Insurance (~1–3%/yr) pays out if the PG is called. Cost it in on a first deal.
  • 07
    Transfer the "hidden problem" risk
    Strong warranties & indemnities, a deferred seller loan you can offset if they breach, and W&I insurance so an insurer — not you — carries a surprise.
  • 08
    Don't over-leverage
    A comfortable debt-service cover (Memo 01's margin rule) means the guarantee never gets called in the first place. The best protection of all.
The honest limit

As first-time buyers you'll almost certainly have to give some PG — you can't eliminate it. And never move assets to dodge a guarantee you've already signed; that's defrauding creditors. Legitimate protection is: cap it, insure it, minimise the bank layer, and keep your home out — all agreed before you sign.

02

A deal team for less

"how do we get a team cheaply?"

Cheap means right-sized and well-scoped, not skimping on the parts that protect you.

  • 01
    Right-size the team
    The core three only (solicitor, accountant, broker). Skip the corporate-finance lead adviser on a sub-£2m deal.
  • 02
    Regional firms, not City / Big-4
    A good regional M&A solicitor and accountant cost a fraction of London rates for an SME deal — and know the local market.
  • 03
    Fixed-fee, capped quotes
    Get 2–3 quotes; insist on fixed or capped fees; avoid open-ended hourly billing.
  • 04
    Do the workstreams you can
    Commercial & operational DD is yours anyway. Organise a tidy data room so advisers bill fewer hours.
  • 05
    Scope tightly
    Light-touch DD for a small, clean business; set materiality thresholds; spend the budget on deal-breakers first.
  • 06
    Shift risk with warranties
    Cover low-risk items with seller warranties rather than paying to investigate everything exhaustively.
  • 07
    Specialists only when triggered
    Surveyor / environmental / pensions actuary — only if that specific risk actually appears.
  • 08
    Signal a pipeline
    Tell advisers you're a repeat buyer (buy-and-build) — they'll sharpen rates for ongoing work.
The one place NOT to cheap out

The SPA and legal core. A weak agreement is how you inherit a lawsuit — it costs many times any fee you saved. Trim everywhere else; never here.

03

Minimise cash up front

"how do we keep our own money in?"

Two costs hit up front: any buyer contribution, and the fees payable win-or-lose. Attack both with structure and staging.

  • 01
    Max seller finance + investor
    The more the seller leaves in and the investor funds, the smaller the deposit and the loan — and the less of your cash is needed.
  • 02
    Investor funds the equity slice
    Your own contribution can approach zero — though lenders often want some operator skin-in-the-game, so negotiate the balance.
  • 03
    Fund the fees, don't pay them
    Structure the raise (or the facility, where the lender allows) to cover transaction costs, not just the purchase price.
  • 04
    Deferred / success-based fees
    Brokers are often lender- or success-paid; ask advisers to take part of their fee on completion, out of proceeds.
  • 05
    Kill abort-cost risk
    The real up-front danger is fees on a deal that dies in DD. Screen cheaply first (Rule Book → Scorecard → First Call) so you only spend real money on deals likely to complete.
  • 06
    Stage the diligence
    Run the cheap deal-breaker checks first; commit to expensive full DD and legals only once you're confident it'll close.
  • 07
    Get exclusivity & capped aborts
    A lock-out period stops you competing; negotiate capped or deferred abort fees before you start spending.
The honest limit

Lenders usually want some buyer contribution, and a chunk of fees is payable whether or not the deal completes. Budget a realistic minimum — a few thousand in early costs even on deals that fall through — rather than expecting truly £0.

04

Pull the one lever

Do this and all three improve together

Tilt every deal toward more seller finance and investor equity, and the smallest bank loan the affordability allows. The same move shrinks your personal guarantee, lowers your deposit, and cushions the risk — one decision, three wins. Then screen cheaply and stage your spend so you only ever pay real money on a deal that's going to complete.

Smaller bank loan → smaller PG More seller/investor → less cash Cheap screening → fewer aborts

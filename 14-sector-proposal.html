Investment committee — UK roll-up sector screen

Sector Proposal

Recommendation to the committee

Buy the boring compliance annuity — and the most fragmented sector in Britain.

Screening UK service sectors against fragmentation, recurring demand, succession supply, durability, scale-logic and — weighted heaviest for a first-time, debt-funded buyer — entry value and ease of acquisition, two theses dominate: regulation-driven compliance services and commercial grounds maintenance.

The winning pattern is "built by regulation, bought by everyone": mandated, non-discretionary inspections that recur forever, sold by thousands of ageing owner-operators at 3–5×, that consolidators buy at 8–11×. Enter the cool corners of it — before PE bids the small end up.

02

The recommended plays

Pick 1

Compliance testing & inspection

buy ~3–5× · exit 8–11×

The purest expression of the thesis. Law forces the customer to buy — fixed-wire electrical testing (EICR), PAT, lifting-equipment inspection (LOLER), water hygiene & legionella (ACoP L8), fire equipment servicing. Miss it and you're breaking regulations, so demand is non-discretionary and recurs on a legal clock. The market is enormous and barely concentrated — the global TICC market tops £200bn with the top ten holding only ~12%.

Fragmentation: extremeRevenue: mandated, recurringPE: actively building platforms (Complii, Axis CLC)

Your edge: fixed-wire/EICR and PAT sit squarely in David's electrical wheelhouse — you can judge quality and run it. Accreditations (UKAS, NICEIC) are transferable moats.

Watch: the hot corners (fire, water hygiene) already draw PE — enter the cooler niches (LOLER, fixed-wire, backflow) where the small end is still cheap and uncontested, then ride the compliance wave up.

Pick 2

Commercial grounds maintenance

buy ~3–4× · quality 7–9×

The easiest sector in Britain to actually buy into: 21,359 firms, the largest holding just ~1.3%. Commercial contracts (not domestic gardening) are subscription-like — multi-year, annual price escalators, recurring monthly revenue — and the sector is growing 4.6–7% a year. Value sits in the contract book and the crews, not a founder, so it's genuinely manager-runnable (good for over-and-back).

Targets: 21,359 UK firmsContracts: multi-year, escalatingConsolidators: idverde, Ground Control, Nurture

Why it fits: maximum choice of cheap small targets = you can be picky and disciplined on price — exactly what a first-timer needs.

Watch: labour-intensive, seasonal, weather-exposed, moderate margins. Buy the commercial-contract books, avoid one-van domestic gardeners.

Pick 3

Fire & water safety compliance

buy ~4–6× · exit 8–11×

The strongest exit story — a Building Safety Act / post-Grenfell regulatory supercycle, and PE is buying visibly (Ansor/Complii, H.I.G./Axis CLC). Recurring, essential, fragmented. This is the classic "ride the wave" pick: buy a solid small operator and become a bolt-on a platform pays a premium for.

Tailwind: Building Safety ActExit: best-in-classEntry: getting competitive

Watch: because the exit is so clear, the small end is bidding up — you'll compete with well-funded platforms to buy. Better for value if you get in early or via an off-market owner.

03

Second tier & the "not now" list

HVAC / refrigeration servicing
Great business, durable service contracts — but hot and pricey (UK/US multiples ~9.5×; small still 4–7×) and PE-crowded. Fine if you find one off-market cheap; don't overpay into the auction.
Drainage / industrial cleaning
Fragmented, essential, early and cheap — but grittier, more capex (jetting trucks), less clean recurring revenue. A value play for the operationally hands-on.
Domiciliary home care
Huge, cheap (3–5×), ageing-demand tailwind — but CQC-regulated, chronic labour crisis, thin council-funded margins, reputational risk. Attractive on paper, hard to run — especially over-and-back. Not for a first deal.
Pest control · vets · dental
Textbook roll-up traits, but already consolidated — Rentokil/Anticimex, IVC, Bupa got there. Entry is expensive and competitive; late to the party.
04

The committee's steer for this buyer

If value & ease decide it

Lead with Pick 1 in David's electrical lane — a small fixed-wire / EICR / PAT testing business, or a LOLER lifting-inspection firm. You get: mandated recurring revenue, extreme fragmentation (easy to buy, cheap to enter), a transferable-accreditation moat, an operating edge you actually have, and a live PE exit forming above you. It scores top on both value and ease — the two things that matter most on a first, debt-funded deal.

Back it up with Pick 2 (grounds maintenance) as your widest, cheapest hunting ground if compliance targets are thin in your patch — 21,000 firms means you'll always find one, and you can be ruthless on price.

Analyst's honest caveat

Multiples and "heat" move quarter to quarter; treat the scores as directional, not gospel, and re-check current activity before you commit. And remember the meta-point from the Reality Check: the sector is a tailwind you pick by — the deal is still won on price discipline, business quality and not catching happy ears.

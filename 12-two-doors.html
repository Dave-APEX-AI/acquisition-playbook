Two Front Doors

The whole idea in one line

A business that's not for sale and one that's publicly listed are two completely different approaches: with the first you go to the owner and create the opportunity; with the second you go through a broker and compete for it.

02

Door one · not for sale (off-market)

This is the play the whole playbook is built around — and it's covered in depth in Memo 05 · The Approach. In one line:

The off-market approach

Write to the owner by name, as individual operators offering their life's work a safe pair of hands. Lead with legacy and their people, never price. One soft ask, one follow-up, then nurture. Low reply rates are normal — you only need a handful of real conversations. You are creating an opportunity that doesn't exist yet.

Full letter, email, call opener and sequence: Memo 05. This memo focuses on the door most people don't know how to work — the listed one.

03

Door two · publicly for sale (on-market)

Here the business wants a buyer, but a broker guards the gate and other buyers are circling. Your job is to look serious fast, get behind the curtain, and run the process well — without catching auction fever.

How the process runs

1
Find & register interest
On the portals or via a broker. The listing is anonymous — sector, region, rough turnover, a reference number.
2
Sign an NDA
Standard, to unlock the details. Read it (nothing that unreasonably restricts you), then it releases the pack.
3
Receive the Information Memorandum (IM)
The sales pack — company name revealed, financials, the story. Remember: it's a sales document, written to flatter.
4
Questions, then a meeting / viewing
A call with the broker, then ideally the owner and the site. Run your First-Call playbook (Memo 07) once you meet the owner.
5
Indicative (non-binding) offer
A short letter: rough price/range, structure, key conditions (subject to DD & funding), timeline, and why you. Non-binding — it gets you to the table.
6
Exclusivity → DD → completion
If accepted, a lock-out period, then the same diligence, legals and funding as any deal (Memos 08–09).

Getting past the gatekeeper

Brokers work for the seller and screen buyers hard — they prioritise people who look serious and able to close, and quietly ignore tyre-kickers. As honest first-timers, you win them over with clarity and professionalism, not bravado: a clear buying thesis, sector knowledge, a real funding route, and fast, courteous responses. Keep the broker warm — they control access and can favour you.

Enquiry to a brokershort · serious · honest

Hi [broker], I'd like to look at [listing / ref].

Quick background: my business partner and I are actively looking to acquire and personally run one established [sector] business, funding route identified (Growth Guarantee Scheme + seller finance), and we can move properly on the right one. Could you send the NDA and information memorandum so we can take a serious look? Happy to jump on a call.

Thanks, [name] · [phone]

Keep it truthful — "funding route identified", not "cash ready". Brokers spot bluffing instantly, and honesty from a credible first-timer beats a hollow boast.

On-market watch-outs
  • The IM has happy ears built in — it's marketing. Verify everything (Memo 11).
  • Competition inflates price — set your walk-away number before you engage, and hold it. Don't get auction fever.
  • The broker isn't on your side — they're paid by the seller to get the highest price. Courteous, not confiding.
  • Stale & overpriced listings are common — a business sitting unsold for a year is a signal (see the smart play below).
04

The smart play — work both doors

Run them in parallel

Off-market is your edge — the best deals at the best prices, less competition. On-market is your training ground and deal flow — it teaches you the process, brokers, IMs and offers on live deals, and occasionally throws up a gem.

Spend the majority of your effort off-market (where the value is), but actively work on-market listings that fit the buy box — especially for your first deal, where learning the mechanics on a real transaction is worth a lot.

The hybrid worth knowing

A listing that's sat unsold for 6–12 months is a motivated seller hiding in plain sight — the broker process has failed them. Approach it with off-market patience and warmth (legacy, their people, a fair deal that completes) rather than as a competitive bid, and you often get off-market terms on an on-market business.

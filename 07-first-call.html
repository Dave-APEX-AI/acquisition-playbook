Acquisition Memo · No. 07 — The First Call
Buyer's playbook · 14 Sep 2026
What to run when an owner says "tell me more"

The First Call

The whole call in one line

This call is not a negotiation. You're here to listen, build trust, and qualify — and to earn a second conversation and the accounts. Never mention price. Listen 80%, talk 20%.

01

What "a good call" achieves

Four things — nothing more. If you get these, the call was a success, whatever else happened.

Rapport
They liked you and felt heard. Trust is the whole game with this seller.
Qualify the seller
Are they a real, motivated seller — or just flattered and curious?
Qualify the business
Does it match the Rule Book — especially, does it run without them?
A next step
Agree the accounts + a follow-up (ideally a visit). Concrete, dated.
02

Before you dial — 10 minutes

  • Read their Companies House — officers, incorporation date, charges, filing history (Rule Book §02).
  • Skim the balance-sheet trend and their website / team page. Pre-score Stage A+B on the Scorecard.
  • Have two genuine, specific things you admire about the business ready to say.
  • Know your one honest line: first acquisition, doing it properly, funding route identified.
  • Quiet room, the call sheet printed, 45 minutes clear, no rush.
03

The flow — roughly 30–45 minutes

0–3 min
Open & disarm
Thank them for the time. "I'm not a broker, there's no agenda today — I just wanted to get to know you and what you've built." Lower the temperature immediately.
3–15 min
Their story — let them talk
"Tell me how it all started." Then get out of the way. This builds rapport and teaches you the business. Warm follow-ups only.
15–28 min
How it runs & who they are
Gently work through the question bank — how the business runs day-to-day (the big one), customers, team, and what's got them thinking about the future.
28–35 min
Reassure on fit
Surface the two things they fear for: their people and their legacy. Say plainly you'd want to keep the team and build on what they made — not strip it.
35–40 min
Close to a next step
Ask for the accounts, propose a visit, agree who follows up and when. No price, no pressure. Thank them again.
04

The question bank

Not a script — pick what fits the flow. The italic note is what each question is really testing.

How it runs — the owner-dependence testthe one that matters most
  • Walk me through a normal week for you — what do you actually do day to day?Are they the operator, or working on it?
  • Who runs things when you're on holiday?Is there a real second-in-command?
  • If you didn't come in for a month, what would break?The honest owner-dependence answer.
  • How do new customers find you — is that you, or a team/system?Is sales owner-tied?
The work & the customers
  • How much of the work is repeat or on contract vs one-off?Recurring revenue quality.
  • Who's your biggest customer — roughly what share of the work?Concentration risk.
  • How far out does the order / contract book go?Forward visibility.
  • What's grown or gone quiet over the last few years?Trajectory & honesty.
The team
  • Who are the two or three people the business couldn't lose?Key-man risk — and who you'd rely on.
  • How long have the key people been with you?Stability of the team you're buying.
Them & their motivationread this carefully
  • What's got you thinking about the future now?Is the motivation real? Retirement, health, tiredness?
  • What matters most to you in who takes it on?Legacy / staff / price / speed — their real driver.
  • Afterwards — would you want to be fully out, or stay involved for a while?Handover & seller-finance appetite.
  • Have you had conversations with anyone else?Broker involved? Competing buyers?
The numbers — light touch only
  • Roughly what does it turn over, and what does it make once you strip out what you take?SDE / scale — a gauge, not a haggle.
  • Would you be comfortable sharing three years of accounts so I can understand it properly?The real ask of the call.
05

What to listen for — live

Their words score the company for you. Tick these on the call sheet as you hear them.

Green — lean in
  • "The lads run it" · "I'm only in a couple of days"
  • Names a manager / second-in-command without prompting
  • Talks about systems, contracts, repeat customers
  • Customers spread across many names
  • Wants the staff looked after; cares about legacy
  • A genuine, human "why now" (retiring, health, tired)
Red — go careful
  • "The customers deal with me" · "I do all the quotes"
  • "I'm here every day" · can't say who'd cover them
  • One big customer named again and again
  • Evasive on numbers or won't share accounts
  • Only interested in top price; a broker pushing
  • Not actually sure they want to sell — just flattered
06

Handling the tricky moments

If they ask "so what would you pay?"

"Honestly, far too early — I'd need to properly understand the business first, and I'd never insult what you've built with a number plucked from the air. Let me learn it properly, then we can both have a sensible conversation."

Don't — the five ways to blow it

Talk price or throw out a number · over-promise on staff/price · dominate the call · push when they hesitate · pretend you've done this before or that funds are sitting ready. Honesty and patience win this seller.

07

The close & after

Close it warmly, concretely

"This has been really good of you — thank you. Everything stays between us. Could you send three years of accounts when you get a moment? And I'd love to come and see the place — would [day] suit? Either way, no pressure at all."

Within an hour of hanging up: fill in Stage C on the Scorecard while it's fresh, log your notes, and decide the next step — pursue, park, or politely close. A quick thank-you message the same day cements the relationship.

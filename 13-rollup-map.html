Buy where private equity is heading

The Roll-Up Map

The whole thesis in one line

Small trades sell for 3–5× profit; consolidated groups sell for 8–18×. Buy into a fragmenting sector before or during private equity's roll-up, and you either sell into that rising tide — or ride it yourself.

01

Why roll-ups mint money — multiple arbitrage

PE buys dozens of small, boring, essential-service businesses cheap, bolts them into one professional group, and sells the whole thing at a far higher multiple. The gap is the profit.

The scale of it

Blackstone paid an estimated ~18.5× EBITDA (~$2.5bn) for the Champions home-services group; Apollo's Apex Service Partners platform reached ~$10bn; and Guild Garage (garage-door installers) sold for ~$800m. Every one is the same trick — aggregate the unglamorous, sell the scale.

03

Your two lenses

Lens 1

Buy what PE wants soon — ride the wave

Buy a solid business in a Stage 1–2 sector, run it well, and become an attractive bolt-on a consolidator buys — or simply sell into multiples that are rising as the wave matures.

For

A clear, hungry buyer pool and a real exit; rising multiples do some of the work for you.

Against

You compete with well-funded PE for the same small deals — entry is pricier and harder, and much of the multiple expansion may already be priced in.

Lens 2

Be early — become the consolidator

Buy in a Stage 3 sector before PE arrives, cheaply, prove you can run it, then bolt on a few more. When the wave reaches your sector in a few years, you own the platform they want.

For

Cheap, uncontested entry; you capture the multiple expansion yourself; biggest upside.

Against

You carry the execution and the wait; no guaranteed exit; you're betting the wave arrives. More risk, more patience.

The sweet spot — the best of both

A Stage 2 / early-Stage-3 sector: you buy cheap-ish and uncontested and there's a visible wave coming to buy your group in 2–4 years. You're early enough to capture the arbitrage, late enough that an exit is realistic. That's where to aim.

04

How to spot a roll-up sector yourself

The pattern behind every one of these. If a sector ticks most of these, PE will come — or you can lead.

  • ✓
    Highly fragmented — thousands of small owner-operators, no dominant player.
  • ✓
    Essential & recurring — often compliance- or necessity-driven (safety, maintenance, health). Demand doesn't switch off.
  • ✓
    Ageing owners, no successors — a steady supply of motivated sellers (your buy box already targets this).
  • ✓
    Boring & hard to disrupt — no tech cliff; the same work in 10 years.
  • ✓
    Scale actually helps — buying power, shared back-office, national accounts, cross-sell.
  • ✓
    Low customer concentration & cash-generative — many small customers, real cash conversion.
05

What this means for you

Your buy box already sits on the curve

Your Tier-1 sectors span it: electrical contracting, fire & security, testing/inspection are mid-wave (Stage 2); grounds/landscaping, specialist cleaning, water hygiene, drainage are pre-cycle (Stage 3). You don't need a new hunting ground — you need to choose where on the curve you want to be.

The honest reality check

Becoming the next £800m platform is the rare, headline outcome — don't buy on that fantasy (see Memo 11). As a first-timer, the realistic wins are: (a) buy one good business, run it well, and be a bolt-on a consolidator pays a premium for; or (b) build a small group of 2–4 over years and sell it at an expanded multiple. Both are excellent. The roll-up lens is a tailwind to pick your sector by — not a get-rich-quick plan.

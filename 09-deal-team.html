Who you hire, what they do, when, and what it costs

The Deal Team

The whole thing in one line

You need a tight core of three — a corporate solicitor, a deal-savvy accountant, and a finance broker — plus specialists only when a specific risk shows up. Their fees are the real cash in a "£0-down" deal, so budget them in.

01

The core three — every deal

Hire people who do SME acquisitions every week, not your local high-street firm. Get fixed-fee or capped quotes where you can, and appoint them once you've agreed Heads of Terms.

Corporate / M&A solicitor

£15k–£40k+

Runs the legal side end to end: sets up your Newco, drafts and negotiates the Share Purchase Agreement (warranties & indemnities that put historic risk on the seller), the disclosure process, the seller-loan agreement, the shareholders' agreement with your investor, and reviews the lender's security & personal-guarantee documents. Also leads legal due diligence.

When: Heads of Terms → completionOwns: SPA, legal DD, security
Non-negotiable. This is the one you never cut corners on — a weak SPA is how you inherit a lawsuit.

Accountant — financial & tax due diligence

£5k–£20k+

Verifies the numbers you're pricing off: a quality-of-earnings review — real profit vs reported, which add-backs are genuine, working-capital needs, tax exposures. Advises share vs asset purchase, the tax-efficient structure, and builds the model the lender needs. Often the same firm handles tax.

When: due-diligence stageOwns: financial + tax DD, the model
Use one experienced in acquisitions — a bookkeeper who files your VAT is not the same thing.

Acquisition-finance broker

fee or ~1–2% of facility

Sources and packages the debt: which GGS-accredited lenders back first-time buyers, how to structure GGS + seller loan + equity, and negotiating rates and PG terms on your behalf. Worth it as first-timers — they speak the lenders' language.

When: funding stageOwns: the debt raise
Some are lender-paid; always ask how they're remunerated so you know whose side they're on.
02

Due diligence — the workstreams & who owns each

"Due diligence" isn't one person — it's several parallel investigations. For a small deal your core three cover most of it; specialists slot in only where a risk is flagged.

WorkstreamWho owns itWhat it checks
Financial DDAccountantReal profit & margins, add-backs, cash conversion, working capital, debtor/creditor health, forecasts.
Tax DDAccountant / tax adviserPAYE/VAT/CT compliance, hidden liabilities, the most tax-efficient deal structure, stamp duty (0.5% on shares).
Legal DDSolicitorContracts, customer & supplier terms, the lease, employment/TUPE, litigation, IP, licences, change-of-control clauses.
Commercial DDYou (or a consultant)Market, customer concentration, competitive position, why customers stay, pipeline — much of this comes from the First Call & the accounts.
Operational DDYouDoes it truly run without the owner? Systems, the team, key-person risk — the heart of the Rule Book.
Diligence is insurance, not a cost

£10–30k of DD that stops you buying a business with a hidden pension deficit, a fake profit, or one customer about to leave is the cheapest money in the whole deal. Never skip it to save a few thousand.

03

Situational specialists — only when triggered

Don't hire these by default. Bring them in only if the specific risk appears.

Chartered surveyor

If freehold or a material lease

Property valuation, dilapidations survey, lease review — real end-of-lease liabilities that never show on the headline price.

£1k–£5k

Environmental consultant

If an industrial / factory site

Phase 1/2 contamination survey. Contamination is a genuine landmine on old manufacturing sites — and lenders may require it.

£2k–£10k

Pensions actuary

If a defined-benefit scheme exists

A DB pension deficit can dwarf the purchase price. If one exists, treat it as a red flag and get specialist advice before going further.

specialist

Employment / HR adviser

If staff-heavy or complex contracts

TUPE (staff transfer rights), contracts, any restructuring. Usually the solicitor covers it; a specialist for tricky cases.

within legal, or £2k+

Insurance broker

Always worth a conversation

PG insurance (~1–3%/yr of the guaranteed sum), key-person cover, and W&I insurance (transfers warranty risk to an insurer — more common mid-market, increasingly available below).

premiums vary

Corporate-finance lead adviser

Usually overkill sub-£2m

Runs the whole process for you. For a first small deal, the core three + your own effort normally do the job — revisit for larger acquisitions.

Two first-timer tips

1 · Interview for deal experience. Ask any solicitor/accountant how many SME acquisitions they closed last year. The right answer is "lots".

2 · Get abort-cost clarity. Agree what you owe if the deal falls through in DD — some fees are payable regardless. Know it before you start spending.

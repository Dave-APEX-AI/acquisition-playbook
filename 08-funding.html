Funding & the Stack

The whole thing in one line

You don't take out one loan — you stack government-backed bank debt, a seller loan and a little investor equity, sized so the business's own profit repays it. The scheme caps at £2m; affordability caps it lower.

01

How the funding works — the stack

Four layers make up the purchase price. Each covers a slice, which is why you can control a £1m+ business with little of your own cash.

1 · GGS-backed bank term loan
The anchor. A high-street/challenger lender funds the bulk; the government guarantees 70% of it to the lender. Up to 10-year term.
~55–70%
2 · Seller loan (deferred consideration)
The seller leaves part of the price in, repaid monthly from profit over ~3–5 yrs. Usually unsecured & subordinated.
~20–40%
3 · Investor equity
A minority partner puts in cash for a stake. No repayment, no guarantee — they take equity risk.
~10–20%
4 · Your cash + fees
Aim low, but expect a lender to want some buyer contribution, and budget £30–100k of diligence & legal fees regardless.
low
Also GGS-eligible

Asset finance (for vehicles/kit) and invoice finance (against the debtor book) can sit alongside the term loan — useful for the working capital a business needs the day after you own it.

The number that actually matters

£2m is the ceiling, not the offer. A lender sizes the loan to affordability — how comfortably the profit services it (they like debt-service cover ≥1.5–2×). In practice senior debt lands around 2–3× adjusted profit. So on £400k profit you might get ~£800k–£1.2m of bank debt even though the cap is £2m — the rest of the price is filled by the seller loan and investor. The £2m only bites on bigger businesses (£700k+ profit).

£2m is per GROUP — remember for buy-and-build

Every business you bolt on shares the same £2m GGS ceiling across the group. Deal two and beyond usually need commercial acquisition finance on top of, or instead of, GGS.

04

The personal guarantee — how it works

The big one. Understand this before you sign anything — it's where "the business owes the money" becomes "we owe the money".

What it is
A personal promise to repay the bank loan if the company (your Newco) can't. The company is 100% liable; the PG extends that liability to you personally on default.
The 70% myth
The government guarantee protects the lender, not you. It does not reduce what you owe — you're still on the hook for 100%, and the PG is how the lender reaches your personal assets.
Your home is safe
Under GGS a lender cannot take your principal private residence as security. Other personal assets (savings, other property) can be at risk up to the guarantee.
Limited vs unlimited
Limited = capped at a % of the facility or a fixed sum (some lenders as low as ~20%). Unlimited = the full outstanding balance + interest + costs. Always push for a capped, limited PG.
You & your partner
Both may be asked to sign. "Joint & several" means the lender can pursue either of you for the whole amount — try to negotiate several liability (each capped to your share).
What it attaches to
Normally just the bank debt. The seller loan is usually unsecured/subordinated (no PG); the investor takes equity risk (no PG). So the PG covers one layer, not the whole price.
You can insure it
Personal Guarantee Insurance pays out if the guarantee is called after insolvency — premiums ~1–3%/yr of the guaranteed sum. Worth costing in on a first deal.
Expect one
As first-time buyers with no track record, a GGS lender will very likely want a PG on an acquisition. Budget for it — capped, home-protected, ideally insured — rather than hoping to avoid it.
05

How we actually go about it

FIRST
Get the deal shape
You need a target, an indicative price, and the seller's openness to leaving money in — lenders fund a structure, not an idea.
THEN
Use an acquisition-finance broker
As first-timers this is worth it — they know which GGS lenders back first-time acquirers and how to package GGS + seller loan + equity. Accredited lenders incl. NatWest, Lloyds, HSBC, Allica, ThinCats, Funding Circle.
THEY ASSESS
Affordability & you
3 years of accounts, debt-service cover, the deal structure, and your credibility/character. Get an indication → credit approval → formal offer.
EARLY
Line up the investor in parallel
Have the equity slice ready so the stack is complete when the lender says yes.
One honest caveat

These figures are illustrative to show how the pieces fit — not a quote or advice. Rates, appetite and PG terms vary a lot by lender and by how strong the target's cashflow is. Get a broker and take professional advice before committing to any facility.
